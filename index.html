New Orleans is one of the most historically rich cities in America. Here are 10 facts that make it unlike anywhere else.

01
Founded in 1718
New Orleans was founded by French colonists and named after the Duke of Orléans. It's one of the oldest cities in the United States, predating American independence by nearly 60 years.
02
The Louisiana Purchase (1803)
The US acquired New Orleans and the entire Louisiana Territory from Napoleon for just $15 million — roughly 3 cents per acre — doubling the size of the young nation overnight.
03
Birthplace of Jazz
New Orleans is widely recognized as the birthplace of jazz music. The genre emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries from the city's unique blend of African, Caribbean, and European musical traditions.
04
Below Sea Level
Much of New Orleans sits below sea level — some areas as low as 6 feet below. The city is protected by an extensive system of levees and pumps, the largest drainage system in the world.
05
Mardi Gras Dates Back to 1699
French explorers celebrated the first Mardi Gras near present-day New Orleans in 1699. The tradition grew into the massive, world-famous festival it is today, drawing over a million visitors each year.
06
The Only City with Parishes
Louisiana is the only US state that uses "parishes" instead of counties — a holdover from its French and Spanish Catholic colonial roots. New Orleans sits in Orleans Parish.
07
A Melting Pot of Cultures
New Orleans has been shaped by French, Spanish, African, Native American, Caribbean, and American influences. This unique cultural blend gave birth to Creole cuisine, architecture, music, and language found nowhere else on earth.
08
The Battle of New Orleans (1815)
Andrew Jackson's forces won a decisive victory against British troops here — ironically two weeks after the War of 1812 had already officially ended. The battle made Jackson a national hero and future president.
09
Hurricane Katrina (2005)
Hurricane Katrina devastated New Orleans when levees failed, flooding 80% of the city. Over 1,800 lives were lost. The city's resilient recovery and rebuilding over the following decades became a testament to the spirit of its people.
10
Home of the World's Longest Bridge
The Lake Pontchartrain Causeway stretches nearly 24 miles across Lake Pontchartrain, making it one of the longest bridges over water in the world. On a clear day, you can't see either shore from the middle.

Check the weather before you head out — New Orleans can surprise you! 🌂

📅
What to expect in May
May is warm and humid with temps typically ranging from the low 70s°F at night to the upper 80s°F during the day. Rain is common — afternoon thunderstorms pop up frequently, so an umbrella is always a good idea. It's one of the last comfortable months before the intense summer heat and humidity fully set in. Crowds are lighter than Mardi Gras season, making it a great time to visit.
